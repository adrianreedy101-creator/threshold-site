The real story

In the last decade, I built a company that grew to more than 600 people. Not alone, but with an exceptional team.

Midway through, I made a decision that confused almost everyone.

I stopped focusing on performance and started focusing on the people: their values, alignment, health, wealth, and relationships.

To the outside world, this looked strange. My partners did not understand it. The business world did not have a category for it.

But I kept going. I knew culture was the answer, even when no one else could see it yet. Making money was easy. Making change was what inspired me.

A huge part of our waking lives is spent at work. If we build a values-driven culture in that space, it doesn't just change a team. It quietly reshapes families, communities, and the wider world. That's what has always excited me.

What I didn't realise at the time was that I was training a different muscle: the ability to read people at a fundamental level, to hear what sat beneath the words. I could see the gap between intention and outcome, and I started to notice the single shift that, if made, unlocked everything else.

That's the work this practice is built on. It's what I bring to every report I read and every person I sit with.

The work we do together is simple: I hold up a precise mirror so you can see what's really driving you and choose something different.

Threshold is where that mirror gets its sharpest. Over 90 days, one person at a time, we sit inside the work until the reflection is undeniable.

That's what I bring to Threshold. Not a theory. Not a framework I copied from a book. A hard-won truth I lived through.

Where it began

The question that kept me awake.

When I began working with values and culture inside that company, I saw how much it shifted the way people lived and worked, not just how they performed.

The question that kept me awake wasn't just, "How do we get people to perform at a high level?" It was, "What happens if an entire team operates from a higher, aligned state, and what kind of impact could that have on their families, their communities, and the wider world?"

I saw what happened when individuals held their values clearly and stood in them. The whole culture changed. Performance was still there, but it became a byproduct of alignment, not a substitute for it.

I asked those questions at the beginning of my work in personal values. After years of working closely with people and analysing what truly drives their decisions and behaviours, I found it: lasting change doesn't come from more effort. It comes from accurately understanding what's driving you.

Threshold is where that understanding gets tested, over 90 days, with someone who has done this work and lived it.

The solo climb

I didn't burn out. I outgrew.

Here's what I don't put on a resume:

Every answer led to a deeper question. Every layer of understanding revealed another. I wasn't exhausted from doing too much. I was stretched thin from understanding too much, too fast, with no one else on the same page.

The company kept performing. The culture got stronger. But I was walking a path no one around me could see.

That path required solitude. Not isolation. Solitude. The kind where you sit with what you're learning, embody it, test it, fail with it, and come out the other side not just knowing, but being.

What that produced

I don't teach tactics I've read. I deliver work I've lived.

I don't guess what drives people. I've read hundreds of Personal Values Assessments across my career, and I've lived the messy, beautiful, uncomfortable reality of founding a company that grew to more than 600 people through a values-led transformation.

The result? A company that consistently outperformed. And a methodology that works because it was forged in confusion, not in a classroom.
