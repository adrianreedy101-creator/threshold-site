The real story

In the last decade, I built a company that grew to more than 600 people. Not alone, but with an exceptional team.

Midway through, I made a decision that confused almost everyone.

I stopped focusing on performance and started focusing on the people: their values, alignment, health, wealth, and relationships.

To the outside world, this looked strange. My partners did not understand it. The business world did not have a category for it.

But I kept going. I knew culture was the answer, even when no one else could see it yet. Making money was easy. Making change was what inspired me. And over time, the results proved me right. The company thrived. The people stayed. The performance followed.

What I did not expect was that the philosophy would outlast me. Even after I moved on, the culture I built continued to breathe through the team. That is when I knew it was real.

That is what I bring to Threshold. Not a theory. Not a framework I read in a book. A hard-won truth I lived through.

Where it began

The question that kept me awake.

Why do some people at the top of their game reach a point where more effort produces less? Why do others cross that point and come out the other side lighter, sharper, more themselves?

I asked this question through a decade of scaling a company, and through hundreds of hours sitting with people who had done everything right and still felt something was off. The pattern was clear: the ceiling is never external. It's a set of limits people have quietly agreed to manage, rather than remove.

The solo climb

I didn't burn out. I outgrew.

Here's what I don't put on a resume:

Every answer led to a deeper question. Every upgrade revealed a new ceiling. I wasn't exhausted from doing too much. I was stretched thin from understanding too much, too fast, with no one else on the same page.

The company kept performing. The culture got stronger. But I was walking a path no one around me could see.

That path required solitude. Not isolation. Solitude. The kind where you sit with what you're learning, embody it, test it, fail with it, and come out the other side not just knowing, but being.

What that produced

I don't teach tactics I've read. I deliver work I've lived.

I don't guess what drives people. I've read hundreds of Personal Values Assessments across my career, and I've lived the messy, beautiful, uncomfortable reality of founding a company that grew to more than 600 people through a values-led transformation.

The result? A company that consistently outperformed. And a methodology that works because it was forged in confusion, not in a classroom.

What I learned from the select few

A select group of people have walked this with me. Not because opportunities were scarce.

I've guided a select group of clients through the Threshold 90-day intensive, and I take no more than two people at any time. Not because opportunities are scarce, but because this work is only for those truly ready for it.

The ones who were? They stopped managing their limits and started removing them.

Founders who shifted their relationship to success, and watched their bottom line follow. Executives who repaired marriages they'd quietly given up on. High-performers who stopped using alcohol or adrenaline to manage their internal state.

I don't publish their names. They're available as references after you're accepted into Threshold.
